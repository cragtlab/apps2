THE SOLAR SYSTEM

Eight planets orbit our Sun in a disc-shaped system spanning billions of kilometers. If the Sun were a basketball, Earth would be a marble 26 meters away — and Neptune a marble 800 meters away. Hover over planets for details.

⟶   30 – 100,000 AU   ⟶

Scale: 30 – 100,000 AU

KUIPER BELT & OORT CLOUD

Beyond Neptune lie vast frozen reservoirs. The Kuiper Belt hosts Pluto and thousands of icy worlds. The spherical Oort Cloud extending to 100,000 AU marks the true outer boundary of our solar system and the birthplace of long-period comets.

🟤
Kuiper Belt
30 – 50 AU
Contains Pluto, Eris, Makemake, Haumea — over 100,000 objects wider than 100 km
☁️
Oort Cloud
2,000–100,000 AU
A vast spherical shell of billions of icy bodies — the true gravitational edge of our Sun's influence
☄️
Comets
Timeless Visitors
Gravitational nudges from passing stars launch Oort Cloud objects on million-year journeys inward
🛸
Voyager 1
160+ AU Away
Launched 1977 — the farthest human-made object. Crossed into interstellar space in 2012

Scale: Light-Years

INTERSTELLAR SPACE

Beyond the Oort Cloud lies the staggering void between stars. Our cosmic ruler becomes the light-year — the distance light travels in one year. The nearest star system, Alpha Centauri, is 4.37 light-years away. Even at Voyager's speed, it would take 75,000 years.

📡
Nearest Star System
Proxima Centauri
4.24 light-years away. Has a confirmed exoplanet (Proxima b) in the habitable zone
🌟
One Light-Year
9.46 × 10¹² km
Light travels 299,792 km/s — a light-year equals about 63,241 AU
🚀
Travel Time
~75,000 years
At Voyager 1's speed of ~17 km/s, it would take 75,000 years to reach Alpha Centauri
🌌
Stellar Density
~1 star per 280 ly³
Stars are incredibly far apart — most of the galaxy is empty space between solar systems

THE MILKY WAY

Our home galaxy — a barred spiral containing 200–400 billion stars spanning 100,000 light-years. We live on the Orion Spur, a minor arm about 26,000 light-years from the galactic center, orbiting once every 225 million years.

🌀
Structure
Barred Spiral
Central bar, 4 major spiral arms (Perseus, Scutum-Centaurus, Norma, Sagittarius) plus our Orion spur
⬛
Central Black Hole
Sgr A* — 4M M☉
Sagittarius A* has the mass of 4 million suns. First directly imaged by Event Horizon Telescope in 2022
🕐
Galactic Year
225–250M years
The Sun orbits the galactic center at 220 km/s. We've completed ~20 galactic orbits since our formation
🌑
Dark Matter Halo
~1 Trillion M☉
Invisible dark matter outweighs normal matter ~6:1, forming an enormous spherical halo around us
⭐
Star Count
200–400 Billion
On a dark night you can see only ~5,000 stars. The rest of the Milky Way appears as a cloudy band
📐
Diameter
~100,000 ly
Takes 100,000 years for light to cross. The Sun is 26,000 ly from center — slightly beyond the midway point

Scale: ~10 Million Light-Years

THE LOCAL GROUP

The Milky Way belongs to a gravitationally bound family of ~54 galaxies called the Local Group, spanning ~10 million light-years. Our largest neighbor, the Andromeda Galaxy (M31), is racing toward us at 110 km/s and will collide in ~4.5 billion years.

🌌
Andromeda (M31)
2.537M ly away
The largest galaxy in the Local Group with ~1 trillion stars. Visible naked-eye from dark skies as a faint smudge
💥
The Collision
~4.5 Billion yrs
Milky Way + Andromeda will merge into "Milkomeda" — stars are so sparse that very few will actually collide
✦
Satellite Galaxies
50+ Dwarf Galaxies
LMC and SMC (Magellanic Clouds) are our largest satellites, visible from the Southern Hemisphere
🎯
Group Size
~10 Million ly
All ~54 member galaxies are bound together by gravity and will eventually merge into one giant elliptical
⟶   520 million light-years   ⟶

Scale: ~520 Million Light-Years

LANIAKEA SUPERCLUSTER

The Local Group is just a tiny corner of the Laniakea Supercluster — home to ~100,000 galaxies spread across 520 million light-years. "Laniakea" means "immeasurable heaven" in Hawaiian. All galaxies within flow toward a mysterious mass — the Great Attractor.

🕸️
Cosmic Web
Filaments & Voids
Galaxies aren't randomly scattered — they cluster along vast filaments of dark matter with enormous empty voids between
🎯
Great Attractor
Mysterious Mass
All galaxies in Laniakea flow toward this hidden concentration of mass — partially obscured by our galaxy's disc
🌐
Mass
10¹⁷ Solar Masses
Contains 100,000 large galaxies and countless smaller ones. Discovered in 2014 using galaxy velocity flows
📏
Diameter
520 Million ly
Even at light speed, crossing Laniakea would take 520 million years — 38× the age of the dinosaurs

The Furthest Horizon

THE OBSERVABLE UNIVERSE

The observable universe spans 93 billion light-years — not because the universe ends there, but because that's as far as light has had time to travel since the Big Bang 13.8 billion years ago. It contains an estimated 2 trillion galaxies, each with billions of stars.

🔭
Diameter
93 Billion ly
The universe is 13.8B years old, but expansion stretched the observable sphere to ~46.5B ly in radius
🌌
Galaxy Count
~2 Trillion
Each with billions to trillions of stars. Total star count exceeds all grains of sand on Earth's beaches
📻
CMB Radiation
380,000 yrs old
The afterglow of the Big Bang — our oldest "photo" of the universe. Still detectable as 2.7K microwave glow
💫
Dark Energy
68% of Universe
A mysterious force accelerating cosmic expansion. Normal matter (stars, planets, gas) is only ~5% of everything
❓
Beyond Observable
Possibly Infinite
The actual universe likely extends far beyond what we can see. Some inflation models suggest it is truly infinite
🕳️
Dark Matter
27% of Universe
Invisible, detectable only by gravity. With dark energy, the "dark sector" accounts for ~95% of cosmic content
⟶   from the beginning to now   ⟶

13.8 Billion Years

TIMELINE OF THE UNIVERSE

From a hot, dense singularity to the vast structured cosmos — here is the epic story of everything.

T = 0
🌟 The Big Bang
The universe begins as an infinitely hot, dense singularity. Space, time, and energy spring into existence from a single point.
T = 10⁻³⁵ seconds
💥 Cosmic Inflation
The universe expands exponentially in a tiny fraction of a second, growing from subatomic to macroscopic size and smoothing out quantum fluctuations.
T = 3 minutes
⚛️ Nucleosynthesis
Protons and neutrons fuse to form hydrogen, helium, and trace lithium — the raw material for the first generation of stars.
T = 380,000 years
📻 Cosmic Microwave Background
The universe cools enough for electrons to combine with nuclei. Photons travel freely for the first time — the CMB radiation we detect today.
T = 100 – 500 Million years
🌟 First Stars (Pop III)
The "cosmic dawn" — first massive stars ignite, forging heavy elements and ending the cosmic dark ages with intense ultraviolet radiation.
T = 1 Billion years
🌌 First Galaxies Form
Dark matter halos accumulate gas and stars, assembling into the first galaxies and galaxy clusters through gravitational collapse.
T = 9.2 Billion years
☀️ Our Solar System Forms
A molecular cloud collapses under gravity, forming the Sun and an orbiting disc of gas and dust that becomes the eight planets.
T = 9.8 Billion years
🦠 Life on Earth Begins
Earliest microbial life emerges in Earth's primordial oceans. Over 3.5 billion years, life complexifies from single cells to all we know.
T = 13.8 Billion years — NOW
👁️ The Present Day
You are here — a brief flicker in cosmic time, on a pale blue dot, looking outward across billions of light-years of history frozen in starlight.
T = ~10¹⁴ years (future)
💀 Last Stars Go Dark
The last red dwarf stars exhaust their fuel. The universe enters the Degenerate Era — only white dwarfs, neutron stars, and black holes remain.
