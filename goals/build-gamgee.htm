Hard truth
“Commercial vaccine in 12 months” is not the right promise. The credible year-one outcome is a regulator-aligned feasibility pilot, not an unapproved product sold directly to owners.
01 / the brief

Copy the outcome, not the org chart.

Gamgee presents itself as a software-led, end-to-end platform: it receives a dog’s case, coordinates tissue and healthy-DNA sequencing, identifies mutations, designs a personalised mRNA vaccine, manages production and supports veterinary treatment and response monitoring. Its public site lists UNSW and University of Queensland collaborators and backers including YC, Founders Fund, Golden Gate Ventures, Atypical Ventures and Doogood Foundation.

What is worth competing with

The integrated case-to-treatment workflow: clinician intake, pathology and sample logistics, tumour/normal analysis, candidate ranking, manufacturing hand-off, treatment records and response data.

case orchestrationdata moatvet trustrepeatable QA

What not to pretend yet

Do not claim that a single case proves efficacy, that AI alone makes a safe medicine, or that a Singapore company can skip AVS, licensed veterinary care, ethics review, manufacturing quality or owner consent.

Gamgee’s public claims about tumour reduction, turnaround and cost are company claims; use them as hypotheses to test, not as validated benchmarks.

Current public benchmark

Gamgee now names its first product ze-ets-001 and says it is enrolling an Australian 2026 clinical trial. Its veterinary page describes whole-exome sequencing, AI neoantigen analysis, GMP manufacturing, cold-chain delivery and outcome monitoring, with an indicative four-week timeline based on its first case.

These are Gamgee’s public descriptions and indicative claims, not independently verified clinical performance.
Positioning: “The Singapore and Southeast Asia partner layer for personalised canine cancer treatment.” Start with dogs because the clinical workflow is narrower and the emotional buyer is clear; keep the data and platform architecture extensible, but do not expand into human medicine in year one.

02 / Singapore wedge

Win the coordination gap.

Singapore is a strong regional base for biotech partnerships and capital, but the local dog population alone may not provide enough eligible cases for a fast learning loop. Design for Singapore headquarters plus regional referral and trial partners from day one.

Beachhead customer

Veterinary oncology and referral clinics that want a structured route for difficult, treatment-resistant cases—without creating their own bioinformatics, manufacturing and regulatory operations.

First paid product

A clinic-facing case workflow and review service: eligibility, sample logistics, analysis report, manufacturing coordination and longitudinal outcome record. Charge clinics or a partnered programme—not unapproved direct-to-consumer treatment.

First moat

Consent-backed, standardised longitudinal data linking pathology, mutations, candidate selection, treatment, safety and response. A prettier dashboard is not a moat; a reliable clinical evidence loop is.

03 / technology

The minimum credible stack.

Build the parts that create repeatability and trust. Keep experimental protocols, administration and regulated release with qualified partners. This is a product architecture, not a wet-lab recipe.

1. Case + consent

Owner and vet portal, pathology upload, eligibility checklist, consent versioning, privacy preferences and an audit trail.Output: a complete, reviewable case packet before any sample is sent.

2. Sample chain

Barcode/identity, collection instructions owned by the clinic, courier status, temperature and receipt records, tumour/normal matching and sample rejection rules.Output: no “mystery tube” enters the analysis pipeline.

3. Genomics

Partner-run sequencing and a reproducible pipeline for quality control, alignment, variant calling, annotation and tumour-versus-normal interpretation. Start with a narrow, validated assay choice.Output: versioned mutation evidence with provenance, not an opaque AI score.

4. Candidate ranking

Neoantigen and immunogenicity ranking with canine biology assumptions, confidence scores, human-readable evidence and clinician review. Treat model output as decision support.Output: ranked candidates plus a signed review decision.

5. Design hand-off

Construct/version management, codon-optimisation and formulation specifications owned by the qualified manufacturing partner; release criteria and deviations recorded in the case file.Output: a controlled manufacturing package—not a “generate and ship” button.

6. Clinical record

Licensed-vet treatment schedule, adverse-event capture, imaging/pathology timepoints, response criteria, owner-reported outcomes and consented research export.Output: evidence that can support the next financing and regulatory conversation.

7. Security + ops

Singapore-hosted or appropriately governed data environment, least-privilege access, encryption, backups, model/data lineage, vendor contracts and incident response.Output: an investor- and clinic-ready quality system from the first case.

05 / regulatory reality

Regulatory is a product requirement.

Singapore’s route depends on how the product, trial and activity are classified. Get the answer in writing before taking a dog’s sample or promising treatment. The following is a planning map, not legal or veterinary advice.

AVS first

AVS states that veterinary biologics require a licence to possess/import, and imported veterinary vaccines are evaluated and registered before import. Its published fees include S$45 for a licence to possess veterinary biologics and S$210 for registration, before other costs.

Confirm whether the proposed personalised product, sample, trial and manufacturing arrangement fall within these routes.

Ethics before animals

For scientific use of animals, AVS points to NACLAR requirements and institutional animal-care committees. A scientific pilot should have the right written approval before enrolling or treating animals.

Do not use “owner consent” as a substitute for institutional or regulatory approval.

Licensed care

Veterinary treatment must sit with licensed veterinary professionals and an appropriate clinic. The company can provide software and coordination; it should not present itself as the care provider unless it has the required structure.

Ask counsel and AVS about every cross-border step, including sample export, data transfer and product import.
06 / capital

Raise against de-risking milestones.

Do not pitch “a dog cancer cure.” Pitch a controlled platform that creates clinical evidence, owns workflow/data/IP and can later expand into additional species or indications after proof.

Primary Singapore routes

Startup SG Equity can co-invest alongside qualified third-party investors in Singapore-based deep-tech companies with research-driven IP and global potential. SGInnovate is relevant to early deep tech and biotech. EDBI, ClavystBio/65LAB, Vertex Ventures HC via Vertex Holdings and Novo Holdings Asia are strategic life-science routes, generally after credible scientific, clinical and partner evidence.

Ask for: a warm introduction, partner validation, regulatory navigation and a milestone-linked pre-seed—not only cash.

Infrastructure + non-dilutive routes

NSG Bio Tomorrow describes early-stage biotech support with lab access, mentorship, grants or lab credits and investor-network opportunities. Enterprise Singapore’s Startup SG programmes and university translational programmes are worth screening once the entity and technical lead exist.

Previous NSG Bio/Amgen Golden Ticket calls had deadlines; verify the next intake rather than presenting a closed call as available funding.

07 / the year

12 months, four gates.

Each phase must buy down a different risk. A demo is not a clinical result; a signed MOU is not a usable partnership; and investor interest is not financing.

M0–1

Frame the company. Incorporate Singapore entity; choose the first indication; recruit scientific and veterinary leads; map IP ownership; ask AVS for a classification/pathway meeting; interview 10 vets and 20 owners.

Gate 1: path + champion
M2–3

Make the workflow real. Sign a clinic/referral MOU, genomics partner and manufacturing feasibility partner; build consent, sample tracking and case review; write the pilot protocol and quality plan.

Gate 2: partners + protocol
M4–6

Run dry cases. Process de-identified or historical cases where permitted; measure sample failure, analysis turnaround, candidate-review agreement and cost; collect 10+ qualified live leads; secure pre-seed or a funded pilot.

Stop if no financing
M7–9

Run the permitted feasibility pilot. Enrol only under the approved route; deliver the first partner-led cases if allowed; record safety, turnaround, deviations, owner experience and clinic workload.

Gate 3: evidence
M10–12

Decide scale or stop. Compare actual unit economics and response/safety data against the pre-declared scorecard; renew only if clinics, regulators, manufacturing and investors all support the next indication or geography.

Gate 4: go / stop

Stop or pivot when these fail

Stop the therapeutic build if there is no credible regulatory path by month 3, no clinical champion by month 2, no partner-led manufacturing route by month 4, no qualified cases by month 6, or no committed runway by month 6. Preserve the software/data workflow and consider a veterinary oncology decision-support or research-services company only if the science, safety and economics support that narrower business.
